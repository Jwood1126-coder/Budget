# Copilot Graduates From Assistant to Supervised Coworker

Between late 2026 and 2031, Microsoft Copilot will most likely change from an assistant you prompt into a team of agents you supervise. Each agent will have its own identity. In 2027 Copilot reliably finishes multi-step, day-sized office tasks with human check-ins. In 2028–2029, always-on agents such as Autopilot run recurring processes from start to finish, with human sign-off gates. By 2030–2031 the ERP's own screens matter much less, because Copilot reaches the system of record through connectors and the Model Context Protocol (MCP), the emerging standard for connecting agents to business systems, instead of through a person clicking. Microsoft shipped most of the groundwork during 2026:

- Agent Mode in Word, Excel and PowerPoint (GA April 2026).
- Copilot Cowork for long-running tasks (GA June 2026, billed per use).
- Computer-using agents that operate web pages (GA May 2026).
- Agent 365 and Entra Agent ID for governing agents (GA spring 2026).
- An "Autopilot" background agent, in private preview.

So the open question is less whether these abilities arrive and more how fast a mid-size company like Beckett takes them in. The underlying AI models are improving faster than organizations can absorb them. Frontier agents complete roughly 16-hour tasks half the time but only about 3-hour tasks at 80% reliability. The cost of a given capability falls about 13x a year. Yet independent trials still find modest, task-specific time savings (heavy users spent 31% less time on email, with no change in meeting time) and weak habit formation. I assign rough weights of about 50% to a base scenario, 25% to a faster one driven by AI-accelerated AI research, and 20–25% to a slower one. In every scenario, four things set the pace at a company like Beckett: ERP integration, data permissions, trust from skeptical managers, and adoption habits. A business analyst can own all four. Without someone doing that deliberately, Beckett will likely feel each Microsoft release 12–24 months after it is announced.

> **How to read the evidence.** None of the research team's primary-page fetches worked: microsoft.com, learn.microsoft.com, metr.org and most third-party sites were blocked or failed DNS. **Nearly every figure here therefore comes from search-engine summaries of the cited pages, not full reads.** Re-check dates, prices and statistics against the Microsoft 365 Message Center, the Microsoft 365 roadmap and the primary documents before using them in a decision. The report uses four labels. **Reported fact** means a dated release, a published price or an independent measurement, consistent across sources but read through summaries. **Vendor claim** means a statement by Microsoft or an AI lab about its own product or research. **Forecast** means a third-party prediction (Gartner, IDC, METR extrapolations, Metaculus). **Inference** means this report's own projection. The scenario weights are the research team's judgment, not sourced numbers.

## Microsoft already shipped the agent groundwork in 2026

**Inside the Office apps, Copilot now acts by default.** On 2026-04-22 Microsoft declared Agent Mode in Word, Excel and PowerPoint generally available and made it the default experience. It performs "multi-step, app-native actions directly in your documents, worksheets, and presentations." In Excel that means exploring data, building and explaining an analysis, and editing the workbook directly ([Microsoft Copilot blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/blog/2026/04/22/copilots-agentic-capabilities-in-word-excel-and-powerpoint-are-generally-available/)). *Reported fact.* One limit matters for report-heavy work: a third-party review says that, as of April, a single instruction that orchestrates work across Word, Excel and PowerPoint together was not yet GA ([sagnikbhattacharya.com](https://sagnikbhattacharya.com/blog/copilot-agentic-ga-2026)). The Researcher and Analyst agents have been GA since June 2025. Analyst finds patterns in datasets and explains them in plain language. At launch the two agents shared a cap of 25 queries per month ([Microsoft 365 blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/blog/2025/06/02/researcher-and-analyst-are-now-generally-available-in-microsoft-365-copilot/); [Northwestern IT](https://services.northwestern.edu/TDClient/30/Portal/KB/Article/2876/Using-Researcher-and-Analyst-Agents-in-Microsoft-365-M365-Copilot)).

**The bigger 2026 change is Copilot that keeps working after you stop typing.** Copilot Cowork reached GA on 2026-06-16. It is "a full agentic experience for long-running, multi-tool work" with plugins and Edge browsing. It is **billed per use in Copilot Credits on top of the seat license**, and it is **off by default** until an admin enables it and sets spending limits ([Microsoft Copilot blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/blog/2026/06/16/copilot-cowork-is-now-generally-available/)). On 2026-09-25 Microsoft renamed the Microsoft 365 Copilot app "Microsoft Copilot" and reorganized it into three areas ([Microsoft Partner Center](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/partner-center/announcements/2026-september)):

- **Home** combines Chat, Cowork and the Office apps.
- **Code** "extends solution-building to every knowledge worker."
- **Autopilot** is "a persistent, proactive, and personal agent hosted in the cloud."

One partner reports that Copilot will eventually decide on its own whether a request goes to Chat or to Cowork ([eGroup](https://egroup-us.com/news/copilot-september-2026-update/)). Autopilot first appeared as "Microsoft Scout" at Build in June 2026. It runs continuously in the background under its own governed Entra identity across Teams, Outlook, OneDrive and SharePoint, and it was still in private preview as of late September ([Western Computer](https://www.westerncomputer.com/resources/blog/microsoft-scout-autopilot-agent); [Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/microsoft-copilot/microsoft-introduces-scout-the-always-on-personal-ai-agent/4525534)). Smaller proactive features already exist:

- Scheduled prompts, GA early 2026: up to 10 per user, and most connectors are blocked by a fixed data-loss-prevention policy ([WindowsForum](https://windowsforum.com/threads/scheduled-copilot-prompts-in-microsoft-365-proactive-automation-for-teams.401490/)).
- Agentic triage in Outlook (a Frontier preview since April 2026), which drafts follow-ups to unanswered email and resolves calendar conflicts ([Thurrott](https://www.thurrott.com/cloud/335438/copilot-in-outlook-can-now-proactively-manage-your-inbox-and-calendar)).
- Teams "AI meeting memory files," with targeted release in early November and GA in early December 2026 ([mwpro.co.uk, MC1429018](https://mwpro.co.uk/blog/2026/10/13/mc1429018-microsoft-teams-adds-ai-meeting-memory-files-to-support-copilot-and-facilitator/)).

**Copilot can technically operate a browser-based ERP today, but not reliably enough to leave it unattended.** Copilot Studio's computer-using agents went GA on 2026-05-13. They read live screens by vision instead of relying on scripted selectors ([Microsoft Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/blog/copilot-studio-blog/computer-using-agents-in-microsoft-copilot-studio-are-now-generally-available/4519427)). Secondary coverage quotes Microsoft documentation as expecting **about 80% success on web tasks and about 35% on desktop applications**, at a reported 5 credits per action ([WindowsForum](https://windowsforum.com/news/copilot-studio-computer-use-80-web-35-desktop-success.444913/)). *Vendor claim, second-hand.* Electron, Java, Citrix and most virtualized environments are not supported ([Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/copilot-studio-computer-use-agents-ga-deep-dive)). Cowork can also browse in Edge using the user's own sign-ins, but only after an admin turns on the setting, only while the browser session is active, and never in scheduled tasks ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/copilot/cowork/cowork-local-browser)).

The more durable route bypasses the screen. Federated Copilot connectors, which are built on MCP and fetch live data from the source system, reached GA in spring 2026 ([Message Center RM501120](https://mc.merill.net/message/RM501120)). The October 2026 roadmap adds "federated connectors that can perform create, update, and delete actions in third-party systems" ([LevelUpM365](https://levelupm365.com/2026/10/01/microsoft-365-roadmap-updates-october-2026/)). Microsoft's own ERP shows where this leads. Dynamics 365's ERP MCP server and a Cowork ERP plugin let Copilot query Finance and Operations data with citations and deep links, while respecting each user's role ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics365/fin-ops-core/fin-ops/copilot/use-copilot-cowork-erp); [ERP Today](https://erp.today/microsoft-dynamics-365-agentic-erp-copilot-cowork/)). Sources disagree on the GA date (June vs. September 2026). Beckett's ERP vendor is not named, and the researchers **found no confirmed MCP servers or Copilot integrations for NetSuite, Oracle Fusion, Epicor, Infor or Workday**. For a non-Microsoft ERP, the realistic paths today are therefore:

- a vendor API or MCP endpoint, if one exists;
- a Power Platform premium connector (Power Platform has 1,400+ connectors) ([Automation Atlas](https://automationatlas.io/tools/power-automate/));
- computer use as a fallback.

**Governance and pricing caught up in 2026.** Agent 365, Microsoft's control plane for registering, securing and observing agents, went GA on 2026-05-01. It costs $15/user/month standalone or comes in the Microsoft 365 E7 "Frontier Suite" at about $99/user/month ([M365 Admin](https://m365admin.handsontek.net/microsoft-agent-365-becomes-generally-available-ga/); [SAMexpert](https://samexpert.com/microsoft-365-e7-licensing-guide/)). Entra Agent ID gives each agent a directory identity with a human sponsor, and it reached GA around April–May 2026 ([Big Hat Group](https://www.bighatgroup.com/blog/entra-agent-id-ga-deep-dive/)). The Copilot seat still lists at $30/user/month. Copilot Business, for tenants of up to 300 seats, is $21 ([Microsoft Copilot blog](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/blog/2025/12/02/microsoft-365-copilot-business-the-future-of-work-for-small-businesses/)). Agentic work runs on Copilot Credits at $0.01 each pay-as-you-go ([CloudZero](https://www.cloudzero.com/blog/copilot-studio-pricing/)). On models, Copilot now uses three suppliers:

- OpenAI says GPT-5.6 is the "preferred model" for Word, Excel, PowerPoint, Chat and Cowork ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/09/openai-says-gpt-5-6-is-the-preferred-model-for-microsoft-copilot-amid-breakup-chatter/)).
- Claude Opus 5 was rolling out to the model picker in August ([ProPakistani](https://propakistani.pk/2026/08/28/microsoft-now-supports-claude-across-its-copilot-apps/)).
- Microsoft's own MAI models mostly sit behind "Auto" routing ([Sylvain Jacquemard](https://sylvainjacquemard.blog/2026/07/13/claude-gpt-mai-which-model-copilot-m365/)).

The October 2025 OpenAI restructuring gives Microsoft rights to OpenAI models through 2032, including post-AGI models ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/10/28/openai-for-profit-restructuring-microsoft-stake)). Copilot is therefore unlikely to fall behind the frontier for lack of model access during this five-year window. *Inference.*

**Adoption is still early.** Microsoft reported **more than 30 million paid Copilot seats in July 2026**, up from 15 million in January ([Microsoft FY26 Q4](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/earnings/fy-2026-q4/press-release-webcast)). That is still only about 6–7% of roughly 450 million commercial Microsoft 365 seats ([Directions on Microsoft](https://www.directionsonmicrosoft.com/microsoft-claims-15-million-paid-m365-copilot-seats/)).

## The engine doubles every four months, but reliability lags fivefold

**Raw capability is no longer the bottleneck for most office tasks. Reliability is.** METR measures how long a task (in expert human time) an AI agent can complete. In January 2026 METR put the post-2023 doubling time at **131 days**, compared with about 7 months over 2019–2025 ([METR, Time Horizon 1.1](https://metr.org/blog/2026-1-29-time-horizon-1-1/)). By spring 2026 the best model (Claude Mythos) reached a **50%-success horizon of at least 16 hours, but an 80%-success horizon of only about 3 hours** ([Wikipedia: METR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METR)). *Reported measurement, secondary.* Three caveats keep this from being a crystal ball:

- An October 2026 statistical critique puts error bars at about 2x in either direction ([arXiv 2610.12466](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.12466v1)).
- METR says its task suite saturates above about 16 hours.
- The tasks are software and research tasks, not spreadsheets or email.

The **fivefold gap between "works half the time" and "works four times in five"** is the most important number for anyone planning enterprise use. Business processes need 80–99% reliability, which arrives a year or more after the headline capability. *Inference.*

**Office-specific benchmarks tell the same story: strong on single deliverables, weak on whole jobs.** GDPval has industry experts grade AI work products blind against professionals' work in 44 occupations. The best score rose from **47.6% win-or-tie in September 2025** ([GDPval paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.04374v1)) to a reported **80–85% in April 2026** for GPT-5.5 and Claude Opus 4.7 ([BenchmarkList](https://benchmarklist.com/benchmarks/openai_gdpval/)). *Lab claims, not independently replicated.* On the OSWorld desktop benchmark, agents passed the 72% human baseline in early 2026, and top scaffolds now report 83–90% ([presenc.ai](https://presenc.ai/research/osworld-computer-use-leaderboard-2026)). The harder OSWorld 2.0 shows only **about 44% full completion** against a human baseline of about 70% ([BenchLeader](https://www.benchleader.com/benchmarks/osworld_2)). The Remote Labor Index tested real freelance projects and found the best agent fully automated just **2.5%** of them in late 2025 ([arXiv 2510.26787](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.26787)). The pattern matters for Beckett. Copilot can already draft a variance memo or build a pivot analysis at professional quality most of the time. It cannot yet own a messy, multi-system month-end close.

**Falling costs determine when frontier ability reaches a flat-priced seat.** Epoch AI measures the price of reaching a fixed performance level falling about **47% per quarter, roughly 13x a year**, and falling fastest at the frontier ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/publications/the-plunging-price-of-thought)). Long agent runs consume many more tokens, which offsets part of that decline. My inference is that capabilities premium-priced in Cowork or Copilot Credits today will migrate into the standard seat on a **12–24 month lag**. Microsoft has the capacity to supply this. Quarterly capex was about $41B in the quarter ending June 2026, with more than $50B guided for the next quarter, and Azure remains capacity-constrained ([CFO Dive](https://www.cfodive.com/news/microsoft-holds-line-ai-spending-plans/826648/); [Quartz](https://qz.com/microsoft-earnings-azure-100-billion-cloud-073026)).

**Lab predictions cluster inside the five-year window. Their track record argues for discounting them by a year or two.** Altman set goals of an "automated AI research intern by September 2026" and a "true automated AI researcher by March 2028" ([Sam Altman on X](https://x.com/sama/status/1983584366547829073)). OpenAI says it met the intern goal in September 2026. It defined and graded the milestone itself, and **more than half of the successful 4–8 hour tasks needed at least one human intervention** ([GearLive](https://www.gearlive.com/news/article/openai-automated-research-intern-milestone)). Amodei's January 2026 essay says AI able to do most or all jobs is "much less than five years" away ([darioamodei.com](https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology)). Hassabis bets on AGI around 2030. He names continual learning, long-horizon planning and structured long-term memory as missing pieces, and gives about 50/50 odds that new algorithmic ideas are still needed ([CO/AI summary](https://getcoai.com/clear-channel/20vc-deepminds-demis-hassabis-on-agi-scaling-laws-and-the-10x-industrial-revolution/)). Independent forecasters are somewhat slower:

- The AI Futures Project's August 2026 medians for fully automated coding range from **November 2027 to January 2030**. The authors judge that reality is moving at about 70–90% of the pace in their "AI 2027" scenario ([AI Futures blog](https://blog.aifutures.org/p/q25-2026-timelines-update-uplift)).
- Metaculus's community median for "weakly general AI" is about **June 2028** ([AIToolsReview](https://aitoolsreview.co.uk/insights/agi-timeline-predictions-2026)).
- Metaculus's median for strong AGI (including robotics) is about **February 2031** ([Metaculus](https://www.metaculus.com/questions/5121/date-of-artificial-general-intelligence/)).

All of these are *forecasts*. The OpenAI intern episode is a useful template for reading them: headline targets get met under generous definitions while real autonomy lags behind.

**Benchmarks overstate near-term productivity.** METR's randomized trial found that experienced developers were **19% slower** with AI in early 2025 while believing they were faster. Its 2026 follow-up was inconclusive ([METR](https://metr.org/blog/2026-02-24-uplift-update/)). The history suggests a **1–2 year lag** between benchmark capability and measured productivity at work. *Inference.* The table converts the trend into planning ranges. The 80% column matters most for unsupervised work.

| Period | Slower (~20–25%): doubling reverts to ~7 months | Base (~50%): 4–5 month doubling to ~2028 | Faster (~25%): ≤4 months plus AI-accelerated research |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mid-2027 | 80% horizon ≈ 1 work-day | 80% ≈ 1–3 work-days | 80% ≈ several work-days; automated coder in labs by late 2027 |
| 2028 | 80% ≈ 4 work-days | 80% ≈ 1 work-week or more | Superhuman AI researcher in labs; model capability no longer the limit |
| 2029 | 80% ≈ 2–3 work-weeks | Multi-week tasks with checkpoints | AI Futures' fastest superintelligence median |
| 2031 | 80% ≈ 2 work-months | Measurement breaks down; constraints are organizational | Constraints are entirely deployment, trust and law |
| Cost per fixed capability | Falls ~5x/yr | ~10–13x/yr | Faster still |

*Source basis: the research team's extrapolation from METR's measurements and the AI Futures and Epoch figures cited above. These are inferences, not published forecasts.*

## A half-year path from late 2026 through 2031

The base case below combines Microsoft's announced direction with the capability trend. Microsoft's own 2025 Work Trend Index describes three phases ([Microsoft WorkLab](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/2025-the-year-the-frontier-firm-is-born)):

1. AI as an assistant.
2. Agents as "digital colleagues" working at human direction.
3. Humans directing agents that run entire processes, so that "every employee becomes an agent boss."

The 2026 edition claims Phase 2 has arrived for a minority, the 16% of AI users it calls "Frontier Professionals," and that organizations, not individuals, are now the bottleneck ([The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/microsofts-2026-work-trend-index-your-employees-figured-out-ai-your-company-didnt/)). *Vendor research.* An independent review notes it leaves out employment effects, tool limitations and independent sources ([Agenda Digitale](https://www.agendadigitale.eu/people-and-change/work-trend-index-microsoft-il-report-sullai-che-dice-molto-ma-non-tutto/)). Microsoft published no dates for Phase 3. The timing below is my inference.

| **H2 2026** (now–Dec) | Agent Mode default in Office; Cowork for multi-step jobs; Teams meeting memory; Autopilot private preview widens; write-capable federated connectors on the roadmap | Reported facts; Ignite 17–20 Nov 2026 ([CX Today](https://www.cxtoday.com/events/microsoft-ignite-2026/)) | Analysts build Excel analyses by instruction; ERP guidance is still "Vision explains the screen" |

| **H2 2027** | Agents reliably finish day-sized tasks (e.g., "pull ERP exceptions, reconcile against email, draft the variance pack") with check-ins; computer use good enough for routine ERP reads | Model 80% horizon ≈ 1–3 days (inference); Gartner predicts 40%+ of agentic projects canceled by end-2027 ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-06-25-gartner-predicts-over-40-percent-of-agentic-ai-projects-will-be-canceled-by-end-of-2027)) | A shakeout year: some pilots die on cost or risk, and the survivors become standard processes |

### Late 2026: Ignite sets the 2027 agenda

Ignite on 17–20 November 2026 is the next decision point. Based on Microsoft's pattern (Build previews, Ignite expands, GA follows within 6–12 months), expect the event to:

- move Autopilot and Cowork browsing toward broader availability;
- add detail to Agent 365 and Copilot Credit pricing;
- give dates for federated connectors that can write to third-party systems.

That expectation is speculation from the pattern. Microsoft has also merged the Dynamics 365 and Power Platform roadmaps into one continuously updated "AI at Work" roadmap, with no 2026 wave 2 release plan ([Dynamics 365 blog](https://microsoft.com/en-us/dynamics-365/blog/business-leader/2026/08/25/one-always-on-roadmap-dynamics-365-power-platform-and-dataverse-join-the-ai-at-work-roadmap/)). Business applications are now planned as part of the Copilot platform. For Beckett's analyst, the practical change this quarter is small but real. Licensed users already have Excel Agent Mode for report analysis and scheduled prompts for recurring email digests. A user can share their screen with Copilot Vision and ask it to explain an ERP screen they are stuck on.

### 2027: Copilot finishes day-sized tasks under supervision

In the base case, 2027 is the year delegation becomes routine for capable users. Tasks of one to a few days reach roughly 80% reliability at the frontier. The 13x annual cost decline puts late-2026 frontier ability within the standard seat's budget by 2028. A typical 2027 workflow: the analyst tells Cowork to pull the week's open purchase orders through a connector, match them against supplier emails, flag mismatches and draft a summary for the director. The analyst then reviews the exceptions instead of building the spreadsheet. Computer use will be good enough for reading information from an ERP but not for unattended posting. The roughly 80% web success rate Microsoft documents today means one transaction in five needs rescue, and that rate has to improve sharply before agents post to ledgers unattended. Late 2027 is also when Gartner's forecast cancellation wave arrives. Organizations without clear value cases, cost controls or risk controls will shut down agent projects, so 2027 rewards companies that start narrow and measure results.

### 2028: Agents get jobs, not just tasks

Around 2028 the unit of delegation changes from a task to a role, such as "monitor the ERP exception queue and chase vendors." Microsoft built the identity and governance pieces for this in 2026: Entra Agent ID with human sponsors, the Agent 365 registry, and Purview and Defender coverage. These exist so that an always-on agent can be given access, audited and switched off like an employee account. In the base case, Autopilot-class agents are GA and affordable for mid-size firms by 2028, and Microsoft's "agent boss" framing becomes a real job description. Gartner's forecast that 15% of day-to-day work decisions will be made autonomously by 2028 is a reasonable upper-middle marker. Its forecast that 70% of enterprises will abandon agentic AI built by vendor forward-deployed engineers by 2028 ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2026-09-29-gartner-predicts-70-percent-of-enterprises-will-abandon-agentic-ai-built-by-vendor-forward-deployed-engineering-by-2028)) favors Beckett building agent capability in-house, starting from the analyst seat, over buying bespoke systems.

### 2029–2031: The ERP screen recedes behind the agent

Nadella argued in December 2024 that business applications are "essentially CRUD databases with a bunch of business logic" (CRUD meaning create, read, update and delete records), and that "all the logic will be in the AI tier" ([CX Today](https://www.cxtoday.com/data-analytics/microsoft-ceo-ai-agents-will-transform-saas-as-we-know-it/)). By 2029–2031 in the base case, that thesis is visible at mid-size firms. People reach most routine ERP work through Copilot or Cowork, which call the ERP's MCP or API endpoints, and the ERP's own interface serves mainly configuration, exceptions and audit. Microsoft's Dynamics 365 is already built for this. Third-party ERPs are moving the same way (SAP's Joule works bidirectionally with Microsoft 365 Copilot ([SAP](https://www.sap.com/canada-fr/products/artificial-intelligence/joule-joule-and-microsoft-365-copilot.html))). Beckett's timing depends on its ERP vendor. A skeptic would rightly note that this is the part of the forecast with the least direct evidence. Today's documented systems are Microsoft's own ERP and SAP. Nothing was found for the mid-market ERP vendors.

### Faster and slower paths diverge after 2027

The scenarios differ less in what Copilot eventually does than in when it can do it unsupervised, and in how hard the change hits jobs.

| Dimension | Slower (~20–25%) | Base (~50%) | Faster (~25%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Driver | Doubling slows to ~7 months; power and data limits after 2028; continual learning unsolved | Trend holds to ~2028, then becomes hard to measure | AI automates AI research (AI Futures assumes 20–32x R&D uplift at the automated-coder milestone) |
| Copilot in 2028 | A "supervised delegate": multi-hour tasks, a human checks everything | Persistent role-based agents with sign-off gates | Autopilot-class agents run whole processes at early adopters; capability limited by governance, not models |
| Copilot in 2031 | Roughly where the base case sits in 2028 | Phase 3 at leading firms; mid-size firms partway there | Phase 3 widespread; the question becomes what humans should still own |
| ERP integration | Computer use stays fragile; API/MCP coverage spotty for mid-market ERPs | Most major ERPs reachable by MCP by ~2029 | Agents operate any UI reliably, so integration stops being a blocker |
| Labor effects | Task reshaping; fewer junior hires | Visible shrinkage in entry-level analyst and coordinator hiring | Amodei-style disruption of entry-level white-collar work within 1–5 years ([darioamodei.com](https://darioamodei.com/essay/the-adolescence-of-technology)) |
| What Beckett feels | Gradual; the Microsoft trust story stays intact | Visible role change by 2028–2029 | Strategic pressure from competitors by 2028, whatever Beckett's own readiness |

Even in the faster scenario, organizations are the throttle. Gartner found that only **5% of organizations that finished a Copilot pilot moved to larger-scale deployment** in 2025 ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6548002)). McKinsey found that no more than 10% of organizations reported scaling agents in any given function ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-state-of-ai-2025)). In the slower scenario, Copilot still improves a great deal. By 2031 it would handle what the base case expects in 2028. Planning for "nothing changes" is the one choice none of the evidence supports.

## Integration, trust, governance and adoption set Beckett's real clock

**Integration is the first wall for ERP use.** Microsoft's own commentators say an API call is "faster, more reliable, and more auditable" than an agent interpreting a screen ([Technspire](https://technspire.com/en/blog/computer-using-agents-hit-ga-when-copilot-studio-ui)). EY frames computer use as the next evolution of RPA rather than a replacement for proper integration ([ESPC webinar](https://espc.tech/learning-hub/webinar/computer-use-in-copilot-studio-the-next-evolution-of-rpa-and-desktop-automation/)). Microsoft's own ERP agents show the constraints:

- Business Central's Sales Order Agent pauses when Copilot Credits run out.
- The Payables Agent validates invoices in English only.
- Reported daily caps are 500 invoices and 100 emails ([Tigunia](https://tigunia.com/blog/business-central-ai-agents-controller-pre-flight-checklist/)).

One critic warns that Dynamics finance agents are "outrunning the audit trail built to explain them" ([RouteGet](https://routeget.com/technology-consulting/dynamics-365s-new-finance-agents-are-outrunning-the-audit-trail-built-to-explain-them/)). For Beckett, whether its ERP vendor offers an API or MCP endpoint decides whether the ERP integration happens in 2027 or not until 2029.

**Governance is the second wall, and it moved from data to agents.** Copilot respects existing permissions, so it surfaces anything a user can technically reach. One analysis put it this way: it "doesn't create oversharing; it makes years of it instantly discoverable" ([comparethecloud](https://www.comparethecloud.net/articles/stop-microsoft-copilot-surfacing-confidential-data-wrong-people-365-tenant)). In Gartner's 2025 survey, **oversharing concerns delayed 40% of rollouts by three months or more**, and 64% of organizations said governance work took significant time and resources ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/6548002)). Agents add new problems:

- A poisoned prompt can redirect outputs and leak personal data without alerts ([TechJack Solutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-tools/microsoft-copilot/securing-copilot-studio-agents/)).
- Purview audit stores a thread ID rather than the conversation text ([PowerTricks](https://www.powertricks.io/monitor-copilot-studio-agents-runtime/)).
- Easy agent creation produces sprawl "faster than any manual review process can track" ([EPC Group](https://www.epcgroup.net/blog/copilot-studio-agent-governance-nine-controls-dependency-order-october-2026)).

Microsoft recommends SharePoint Advanced Management and Purview Data Security Posture Management (DSPM) for AI to find and fix oversharing before agents expose it ([Microsoft Q&A](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5852075/what-governance-access-and-action-controls-can-we)). Identity and the agent registry are GA. Runtime threat protection and full audit fidelity were still partial in mid-2026, so cautious firms will reasonably hold back autonomous agents until 2027–2028.

**Trust depends on reliability and accuracy, and the evidence there is mixed.** The UK Department for Business and Trade trial found 72% of users satisfied. It also found hallucinations, **no evidence of improved productivity**, and that scheduling tasks took *longer* with Copilot ([DBT evaluation](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/68adbe409e1cebdd2c96a19d/dbt-microsoft-365-copilot-evaluation.pdf)). In the Australian Treasury's trial, staff rated Copilot below other generative AI tools they had used, and most used it two or three times a week or less ([Treasury evaluation](https://evaluation.treasury.gov.au/publications/evaluation-generative-artificial-intelligence)). The trust that Beckett's director and fellow analyst give Microsoft is an asset, but it covers the product's security and governance, not its accuracy. Losing it through one bad automated ERP entry would set adoption back further than any missing feature. *Inference.*

**Adoption habits are the quietest bottleneck and the most decisive.** In Gartner's 2025 survey, **72% of organizations struggled to fit Copilot into daily routines and 57% saw engagement fall off quickly** ([techpartner.news](https://www.techpartner.news/news/gartner-microsoft-copilot-hype-offset-by-roi-and-readiness-realities-618118)). Gartner's June 2026 assessment concludes that "wide-scale adoption and value realization is not guaranteed" ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/7974937)). Secondary reporting of a Recon Analytics survey says that where employees have both tools, 76% pick ChatGPT and 18% pick Copilot ([About Chromebooks](https://www.aboutchromebooks.com/most-common-ai-tools-used-at-work/)). That figure is *unverified*. Microsoft's own 2026 telemetry says organizational factors (culture, manager support, rewards) explain **more than twice the AI impact of individual factors (67% vs. 32%)**, and only 13% of workers feel rewarded for AI-driven reinvention ([The Neuron](https://www.theneuron.ai/explainer-articles/microsofts-2026-work-trend-index-your-employees-figured-out-ai-your-company-didnt/)). *Vendor research*, but consistent with the independent trials.

**Cost is a fifth, emerging bottleneck.** Moving from a predictable seat price to seat plus consumption makes budgets less predictable. Third-party estimates put a light Cowork task at $0.70–$2 and a heavy one at $15 or more ([Avantiico](https://avantiico.com/copilot-cowork-pricing-for-businesses-guide/)). *Not Microsoft figures.* A skeptical director will notice a variable bill before any productivity gain. Credit forecasting and spending caps are therefore part of the adoption work, not an afterthought.

## Analysts who design and verify agent work will lead

**The best evidence so far shows Copilot reshaping tasks, not replacing roles.** A six-month randomized trial with 7,137 workers at 66 firms found:

- Regular users cut email time by **3.6 hours a week (31%)**.
- The average effect across everyone given access was **1.3 hours a week**.
- Documents were finished somewhat faster.
- **Meeting time did not change** ([NBER WP 33795](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w33795/w33795.pdf)).

The authors conclude that coordinated gains require organizational changes, not just licenses. The UK Department for Work and Pensions, which used a comparison group, measured **19 minutes saved per day** on routine admin ([Computing](https://www.computing.co.uk/news/2026/government/civil-servants-save-19-minutes-day-copilot/)). One estimate puts Copilot's break-even at about 13 minutes a week for a mid-level public servant ([IDM](https://idm.net.au/node/15043)). The case for the license is easy to make. The case for transformation is not. Microsoft's research on 200,000 Copilot conversations found the highest AI applicability in information gathering, writing and communication. The authors stress that this measures task overlap, not job loss ([arXiv 2507.07935](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.07935)).

**The labor signal shows up in hiring, not layoffs, and it lands on junior office roles.** Stanford's ADP-payroll study found that employment for 22–25-year-olds in the most AI-exposed occupations was **19% below** that of peers by mid-2026, up from 13%. The mechanism is reduced hiring, not more separations, and the authors find no widespread displacement ([Stanford Digital Economy Lab](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/news/canariesaug26/)). Announced US layoffs attributed to AI reached 87,714 through May 2026, 22% of all announced cuts ([Challenger, Gray & Christmas](https://www.challengergray.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Challenger-Report-May-2026.pdf)). Some of those attributions are contested as "AI washing." For a business analyst and coordinator, the base case through 2028 is fewer hours spent drafting, summarizing and navigating screens. More hours go to specifying work, checking agent output, redesigning processes and owning agents. Microsoft describes this as shifting from "doing" to "directing and judging." *Inference, consistent with vendor framing.* Coordination and scheduling are the parts of the job Copilot handles worst today. They will hold up longest in the slower scenario and fall fastest in the faster one, when Autopilot-class agents manage calendars and follow-ups.

**The leadership opening is the gap between what Microsoft ships and what Beckett absorbs.** Gartner favors business-led over IT-led Copilot programs ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/documents/5818647)). A common failure mode is that "no one whose job it was to keep it going" exists ([Quisitive](https://quisitive.com/30-million-seats-and-counting-the-copilot-roi-question-nobody-wants-to-answer/)). Consultancies claim that champion networks of about 2–3 per 50 users lift adoption ([Copilot Consulting](https://copilotconsulting.com/insights/microsoft-copilot-champions-program-internal-advocates)). That is a *vendor claim* with no independent study behind it. With a skeptical director and peer, the persuasive approach is measured evidence, not enthusiasm. The DBT diary study showed that self-reported satisfaction does not prove productivity, and the DWP trial showed what a comparison group adds. The phased plan below is my inference from these sources, fitted to Beckett's situation.

| When | What to do | Why it works with skeptics |
|---|---|---|
| **Now–Q1 2027** | Confirm Beckett's licensing facts. Over 2,000 seats removes in-app Chat for unlicensed users ([SAMexpert](https://samexpert.com/copilot-chat-enterprise-restriction-april-2026/)); 300 or fewer seats qualifies for Copilot Business at $21. Ask the ERP vendor whether it offers APIs or MCP. **The watch list for the next 12 months is short and dated:**

Each item moves a box in the half-year table above, and tracking them is itself a credible first deliverable for a skeptical director.

## Conclusion

The deepest finding is a mismatch in speeds. Model capability compounds roughly 7x a year and its cost falls roughly 13x a year. Organizational absorption moves at the pace of permission cleanups, budget cycles and habit formation. That mismatch means Beckett's experience over the next five years depends less on which scenario the AI labs land in and more on its own readiness work. Readiness work holds its value across all three scenarios:

- clean permissions;
- documented processes with explicit "definitions of done";
- an ERP integration path that is not screen-scraping;
- baseline metrics;
- a named owner for each agent.

In the slower world, that groundwork is what produces any measurable return. In the faster world, it is what lets Beckett absorb capability that arrives faster than anyone can retrain. A plan built on predicting the right scenario is fragile. A plan built on this groundwork pays off in all three.

The second implication suits a skeptical team. Microsoft's competitive advantage is moving from model quality to trust infrastructure: identity, governance, audit and data grounding. Microsoft now buys its frontier models from both OpenAI and Anthropic and builds its own. What differentiates Copilot is the controls that the director already trusts. The analyst who frames AI integration as operating those controls well, rather than as adopting AI, is speaking the skeptics' language. That analyst is also building the skill set (specifying, verifying and governing agent work) that the evidence suggests will matter most to office careers through 2031. The entry-level hiring data adds urgency. Junior analyst and coordinator roles are where the labor market is already adjusting, and they are the roles most likely to be redefined as agent supervision.
