How the personal allocation works

Person A directs an editable amount from each paycheck into a personal allocation. Personal debt targets are paid from that amount. The whole-household plan counts full net income, personal debts and any leftover allowance once.

A joint-account contribution is not the same as full take-home pay. Transfers between household accounts must not be counted as extra income.

Starting cash excludes money already committed to existing bills. Blank balances stay unknown. This model retains all future surplus as cash; moving money into a savings account does not count as spending.

Cash over time

Monthly scenario · not a forecast guarantee

Cash = starting cash + income − spending, debt payments and one-off costs + scenario interest. Monthly savings allocations earmark part of this cash; they are not added again. Negative cash means the plan needs funding or changes, not permission to borrow.

How totals are calculated
  • Use posted date, so totals follow the statement ledger.
  • Count card purchases minus refunds and direct checking bills once.
  • Exclude card repayments and internal transfers from spending. Show debt payments and investment funding separately.
  • Keep original bank categories intact; review labels never rewrite the source.
  • Do not turn transfers, reimbursements, or unknown credits into recurring salary.
